# MEI — 2026-09-03

## Catalyst
Methode Electronics reported fiscal Q2 2026 results on/around 2026-09-02
after the close. Headline revenue actually beat: net sales of $265.4M, up
10.4% YoY, ahead of Wall Street's revenue estimate. But profitability
missed: adjusted EBITDA of $13.7M vs. $15.7M a year ago (down, and a miss
vs. estimate), pressured by one-time expenses, talent investments, and the
dataMate divestiture; adjusted EPS of -$0.22 vs. -$0.20 expected (a 10%
miss). The company reaffirmed full-year FY2026 guidance (~$1.05B revenue at
midpoint) rather than raising it despite the quarterly revenue beat, which
the market read as a signal that margin weakness will persist through the
rest of the year. One source (StockStory) headlines the move as a 22.3%
drop tied to the print; other coverage cites an 8.8-9.1% morning-session
move, suggesting the stock continued sliding intraday beyond the initial
open reaction (consistent with the screener's -15.4% full-session read).

## Sentiment
Mixed-to-bearish. This is a "beat on top line, miss on profitability,
guidance merely reaffirmed not raised" pattern — not a broken business, but
a market that had priced in margin improvement and didn't get it. Coverage
found emphasizes "mixed margin trends" and an "operational transformation"
underway (including a data-center-exposure angle called out by StockStory)
rather than a thesis-breaking event. No analyst downgrades were found in
the searches run (StockStory explicitly notes it did not find formal
downgrade commentary), which cuts against this being read as a structural
red flag by sell-side coverage — but that absence could also just reflect
sparse analyst coverage of a small-cap name rather than analysts being
sanguine.

## Risks
- Small/mid-cap name — likely thinner analyst coverage and thinner float
  than RARE/PSNY, so the move may be exaggerated by lower liquidity; cuts
  both ways (could mean overreaction, could mean it's easy to whipsaw
  further in either direction).
- Margin trajectory is now the open question for the rest of FY2026 —
  reaffirmed (not raised) guidance despite a revenue beat is a soft signal,
  not a clean re-rate lower, so the setup is ambiguous rather than clearly
  bearish or clearly a bounce candidate.
- The reported one-time/talent/divestiture cost drags need to actually
  roll off for margins to recover — no evidence found either way on timing.
- Earnings-day moves like this can whipsaw for 1-2 sessions as the market
  digests details from the earnings call; the -15.4% screener figure vs.
  ~9% early-session figure suggests the sell-off was still building
  through the day, which is not consistent with a "climactic, ready to
  bounce" pattern.
- No specific bull catalyst (e.g. analyst upgrade, buyback announcement,
  data-center-demand commentary treated as a positive by the market) found
  to support a CANDIDATE call.
