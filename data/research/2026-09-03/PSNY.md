# PSNY — 2026-09-03

## Catalyst
Polestar reported Q2/H1 2026 results on 2026-09-03 that missed on revenue
($727M vs. ~$878M expected, down 8% YoY) and, more importantly, slashed its
FY2026 delivery growth guidance from a prior outlook of roughly 10-19%
volume growth (~66,000-71,000 cars) down to just 3-5% growth. The guidance
cut is directly tied to a June 2026 decision by U.S. regulators denying
Polestar authorization to sell model-year-2027-onward vehicles in the U.S.
under the Connected Vehicle Rule (part of the broader crackdown on
Chinese-linked-supply-chain EVs) — effectively a forced U.S. market exit.
H1 2026 vs H1 2025: unit sales up only 0.4% while revenue was down 4.4%,
i.e. selling roughly the same number of cars for meaningfully less money.
Shares fell as much as ~27-29% intraday.

## Sentiment
Bearish. This is a structural, not cyclical, problem — losing regulatory
access to the U.S. market is a multi-year headwind, not a one-quarter miss
that snaps back. Wall Street's average rating going into today was already
"Moderate Sell" with an average price target of $15, i.e. this was not a
name analysts were bullish on before the print, and the reaction has been to
cut delivery guidance materially, not just miss a number. One outlet
(Cantor Fitzgerald, per TipRanks) called the report "bittersweet" — some
positive elements (unit growth flat rather than negative, cost items) —
but no analyst commentary found calling the sell-off overdone or the stock
undervalued after today's move specifically.

## Risks
- Regulatory/political risk is not resolved — the U.S. market exit is a
  standing decision, not a one-time headline; no near-term path found for
  reversal.
- Structurally weaker economics (flat unit growth, falling revenue) suggest
  further guidance cuts are possible, not a one-off reset.
- Long-only constraint matters here: this is a straightforwardly negative,
  not-yet-stabilizing catalyst with no identified overreaction/bounce
  angle in the sourcing — the size of the drop reflects a genuine
  guidance cut, not a panic/liquidity gap.
- EV-sector macro exposure (rates, subsidy policy, China-linked supply
  chain scrutiny broadly) is an ongoing overhang beyond this one event.
- No short-interest/squeeze setup or contrarian analyst call found that
  would support a near-term bounce thesis.
