# CRDO — 2026-09-02

## Catalyst
Credo Technology reported Q1 FY2027 results after the close on Tuesday, Sept. 1: revenue of $479.0M (beat consensus ~$471.8M, up 9.6% QoQ and 114.7% YoY — seventh consecutive quarter of triple-digit YoY growth), adjusted EPS of $1.20 (beat $1.17 consensus). Despite the headline beat, the stock plunged ~20% because GAAP gross margin compressed to 64.5% from 68.2% in the prior quarter, and Q2 guidance implies further margin step-down. Motley Fool and Investing.com both attribute the drop specifically to margin compression concerns plus customer concentration risk against an already-high valuation, not to a growth miss (guidance for Q2 revenue of $525-535M and >85% YoY revenue growth for FY2027 was actually reiterated/strong). This is a clearly sourced, earnings-driven move.

## Sentiment
Mixed: the growth story remains intact and arguably strong (triple-digit YoY growth, raised full-year revenue growth outlook >85% YoY, optical revenue ramp on track for $600M+ in FY2027), but the market's reaction is bearish on margin trajectory and customer concentration. This reads as a "priced-for-perfection stock disappoints on one dimension (margins) and gets punished hard" pattern, similar to MDB today. No specific named analyst downgrades surfaced in search results.

## Risks
- Already-priced move: like MDB, this is a post-earnings reaction — the catalyst has already occurred, so there's real risk of chasing a move that's largely done playing out intraday.
- Margin trajectory risk: guidance implies continued gross margin step-down, a fundamental (not just sentiment) concern that could persist into future quarters.
- Customer concentration: explicitly flagged in coverage as a risk factor compounding the selloff — a small number of customers likely represent an outsized share of revenue, which is a real structural risk for a name with hyperscaler/optical-networking customers.
- Valuation: stock was already richly valued heading into the print, leaving little room for imperfect execution.
- No specific evidence found of a liquidity/thin-float problem — CRDO is a well-covered, actively traded Nasdaq name with earnings covered across many major outlets within hours, so the -20% appears to be a normal (if outsized) liquid post-earnings reaction rather than a data/volume anomaly.
