## Ticker identity check (done first, per instructions)
**CUE on Nasdaq is Cue Biopharma, Inc. — NOT Cue Health.** These are two
completely unrelated companies that happen to share a "Cue" brand name:
- Cue Health (home COVID-test maker, ticker was HLTHQ after Nasdaq
  delisting) filed for bankruptcy in 2024, was delisted from Nasdaq
  effective 2024-07-08, and has not traded on a major exchange since —
  it is not the source of any legitimate "+/-X% today" move.
- **Cue Biopharma, Inc. (NASDAQ: CUE)** is the actual live-trading
  company behind this ticker — a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical
  company. This is a real operating company's common stock on a major
  exchange, not a shell/SPAC/warrant/ETF wrapper. Identity confirmed;
  proceeding on that basis. (The screening tag's parenthetical "possibly
  Cue Health" was a reasonable initial flag given the name collision,

## Catalyst
Cue Biopharma announced positive topline Phase 2 data (in China) for
CUE-221 in chronic spontaneous urticaria on 2026-09-20/21: at the
highest dose, CUE-221 achieved a 54% complete hive-resolution rate at
week 12 vs. 41% for Xolair (omalizumab), the standard of care. This is a
real, dated, company-specific clinical catalyst — not manufactured.
However, the reported price action is NOT a clean rally: shares reportedly
surged over 40% intraday on the data, then gave back more than 27%
intraday as traders took profits and raised funding-risk concerns,
before settling at the reported -23.70% net close ($28.27) for the
09-18→09-21 window this screen is checking. B. Riley initiated coverage
with a Buy/$83 PT on 09-18, ahead of the data.

## Sentiment
Whipsawed, not simply bullish or bearish. The clinical data itself reads
positively (beat an approved comparator on the primary endpoint), but
the market's actual verdict on the session was a sharp net decline —
consistent with profit-taking after an initial spike plus real concern
about how a clinical-stage company funds itself post-catalyst (dilution
risk via a likely follow-on raise is a common pattern after a good
Phase 2 print in a cash-burning biotech).

## Risks

  raise) is explicitly what traders are citing for the reversal.

- Thin-float/small-cap biotech dynamics (high volatility, headline-driven)
