# VOD.L — 2026-09-06

## Catalyst
On Friday 2026-09-04, Goldman Sachs published a rare "double upgrade" on
Vodafone Group, moving straight from Sell to Buy and lifting its price
target from 85p to 155p — a near-doubling of the target (both figures are
in pence; 85p = £0.85, 155p = £1.55). Analyst Andrew Lee's stated rationale
is that Vodafone's return on invested capital (ROIC) is now accelerating
faster than peers, driven by (a) repair of the UK mobile market (helped by
the completed Vodafone/Three UK merger forming "VodafoneThree") and (b) a
stepped-up cost-cutting programme. Goldman also frames this inside a
broader, sector-wide call — it projects 14% CAGR in European telecom sector
free cash flow 2026–2030, the strongest among "defensive" sectors it
covers — so part of this is a sector re-rating call, not solely a
Vodafone-specific story. Shares rose roughly 2–3% on the day of the note
(reports vary 1.8%–3.3%), and the stock's 5-day gain going into the
weekend was reported around 4.6%. Vodafone was also running a Goldman
Sachs-managed share buyback and had just completed the Three UK merger and
a Romania-related acquisition in the same window, which likely added
supportive newsflow alongside the rating change.

## Sentiment
Mixed-to-improving, but NOT a consensus bullish call yet. This is very
much a single-house, high-conviction reversal rather than the market
broadly agreeing:
- Goldman: Buy, 155p target (was Sell, 85p) — the big outlier move.
- New Street Research: also upgraded to Buy from Neutral separately.
- Morgan Stanley: still only Equal Weight, but has raised its target twice
  recently (105p → 115p → 125p) — directionally supportive but far below
  Goldman's 155p and nowhere near a "Buy" conviction.
- Broader analyst panel snapshot found in searches: roughly 3 Buy / 4 Hold
  / 3 Sell, with one aggregated "average" target cited as low as $10.57
  (this looks like a stale/mixed-currency composite and should be treated
  with caution rather than taken at face value).
So: one high-profile, well-reasoned bullish reversal (Goldman) plus a
smaller incremental positive (New Street, Morgan Stanley target hikes),
against a still-cautious wider analyst base that has not moved as far.
The move is genuinely fresh (this week) and the rationale (ROIC inflection
from UK market repair + cost cuts) is a real, checkable fundamental
argument — not just "stock went up so we raised the target." But the
scale of the target jump (nearly 2x) versus how far the rest of the Street
has moved is a real amber flag for "has this already been priced in / is
this an outlier call the market hasn't confirmed."

## Risks
- Germany, Vodafone's largest market, remains structurally weak: FY26
  results showed a net loss of 103,000 German contract customers for the
  year (77,000 in Q4 alone) and contract mobile ARPU fell to €16.48 from
  €17.08 y/y. Barclays cut Vodafone to Equal Weight specifically citing
  "sustained Germany weakness." This is the main thing that could make
  Goldman's ROIC-acceleration thesis wrong.
- Net debt has risen to €25.4bn from €22.4bn — refinancing conditions and
  rate sensitivity are a real balance-sheet risk if the turnaround stalls.
- A previously-flagged first dividend increase in eight years reportedly
  came under pressure as the German rebound stalled — a dividend
  disappointment would cut directly against the "improving fundamentals"
  narrative Goldman is leaning on.
- Single-analyst risk: this is one Goldman call, not a consensus shift.
  If Q3 numbers don't show the ROIC inflection Goldman is calling for, the
  target could get walked back as quickly as it was raised.
- Short-term technical risk: the stock already moved ~3-5% on the news: a
  meaningful part of the immediate reaction may already be priced in for a
  trader entering now rather than on the print itself.
