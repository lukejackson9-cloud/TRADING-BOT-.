## Catalyst
Real, dated, but explicitly speculative/unconfirmed catalyst. Sequence:
- 2026-06-04: IAC changed its legal name to People Incorporated and began
  trading as "PPLI" on Nasdaq. The company owns People Inc. (People,
  Entertainment Weekly, and other media brands) plus a ~27% stake (66.8M
  shares) in MGM Resorts International.
- 2026-09-23: People Incorporated announced it was **withdrawing** its own
  ~$48.30/share cash proposal to buy out MGM's remaining public shares —
  i.e. its own take-private bid for MGM collapsed.
- 2026-09-25 (the session in question): PPLI shares jumped ~11% (some
  sources say "over 11%," closing at "$40.05" per one source vs. the
  brief's $40.00 — a minor, immaterial discrepancy) on a **Wall Street
  Journal report that MGM Resorts is now considering a potential
  takeover bid for People Incorporated** — i.e. the reverse of the
  9/23 news: instead of PPLI trying to buy MGM, MGM may now be
  considering buying PPLI. Per the reporting, "a possible offer could
  emerge within days," but **no transaction has been announced and the
  reported discussions do not establish that MGM will actually make an
  offer.**

## Risks
- This is binary, unresolved M&A speculation, reported by a single
  outlet (WSJ) and only "considering" per secondary coverage — the
  exact kind of report that can evaporate with no deal ever
  materializing (compare lessons.md #5: a real fact is still weaker
  evidence when it's not the company's own confirmed action).
- Whipsaw risk both ways: the underlying relationship between PPLI and
  MGM has just reversed direction twice in 48 hours (PPLI bidding for
  MGM on 9/23's withdrawal, MGM potentially bidding for PPLI by 9/25) —
  a volatile, fast-moving situation that could reverse again on any
  update (confirmation, denial, or silence).
- Stock already moved +11% same-day on the report — per lessons.md #1,
  entering after the pop on unconfirmed speculation compounds the risk
  of buying a story that may not complete.
- No hard financial catalyst (earnings, guidance, a signed deal) — this
  is pure deal speculation with real complexity (cross-holding structure:
  PPLI owns 27% of MGM, so a reverse acquisition would be an unusual,
  legally complex transaction) that increases execution/completion risk
  even if talks are real.
