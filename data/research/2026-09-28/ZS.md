# ZS — 2026-09-28

## Catalyst
Zscaler (Nasdaq: ZS, cloud/cybersecurity) fell -10.06% on 2026-09-25
($214.64 → $193.05). A real, dated, company-specific negative catalyst
was found: the company announced Chief Revenue Officer Mike Rich is
stepping down for personal reasons, with Head of Worldwide Sales Ross
Tackett named as successor effective 2026-10-01. The drop extended an
after-hours decline of ~4% that followed the initial announcement.
Market commentary attributes the sell-off to concern over execution
friction and sales-pipeline disruption during the leadership transition,
compounded by profit-taking after a strong run: ZS had beaten Q4 FY26
estimates on 2026-09-03 (revenue $898.16M vs. $877.36M est., adjusted EPS
$1.19 vs. $1.08 est., +25% YoY revenue growth) and issued raised FY27
guidance, yet the stock had already fallen ~4.1% on the earnings day
itself despite the beat-and-raise — a similar "good numbers, sold
anyway" pattern as the GWRE/HPE/ASO precedents in the trade ledger.

## Sentiment
Bearish/cautious near-term. This is a real, dated, negative
company-specific event (not a rumor, not sector-wide — no broad
cybersecurity-sector move was reported alongside it), so it's a
legitimate explanation for the drop rather than an unexplained move.
Underlying fundamentals (revenue growth, raised guidance) remain intact,
which is the argument for a dip-buy — but leadership-transition risk in
a subscription/enterprise-sales business is a real, not cosmetic,
concern until Tackett's transition is proven out.

## Risks
- **This project is long-only (no shorting, per Hard Risk Rules)** — a
  negative catalyst-driven decline is not itself a buy setup. The only
  way this becomes relevant is as a potential dip-buy, and there is no
  confirmed catalyst (dated, near-term) for a bounce — just an intact
  long-term growth story that was already true before the drop.
  Buying now would be betting the CRO exit is a non-event, with no
  fresh evidence either way yet.
  - Stock already fell on its own earnings beat three weeks earlier
    (9/3, -4.1%), suggesting elevated/priced-for-perfection expectations
    generally — a pattern where good news doesn't hold up well for ZS
    lately.
- Enterprise/subscription sales orgs are genuinely sensitive to
  sales-leadership transitions; near-term quarters can show real
  execution slippage even when the successor is an internal promote.
- No confirmed analyst downgrade found — reaction looks driven by
  uncertainty/profit-taking rather than a reassessment of fundamentals,
  which argues against treating this as a "clean negative catalyst" in
  the CHTR/KSS sense (those had deteriorating fundamentals; ZS's
  fundamentals here are still fine on paper).
