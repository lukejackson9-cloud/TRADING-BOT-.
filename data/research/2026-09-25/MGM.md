# MGM — 2026-09-25

## Catalyst
MGM Resorts International (identity confirmed — the casino/hospitality
operator) fell -11.0% on 2026-09-24 ($37.85 → $33.69) on a real, dated,
company-specific catalyst: People Inc. (Barry Diller's media/holding
company, formerly IAC, ~27% MGM shareholder since 2020) withdrew its
June-2026 proposal to buy all remaining MGM shares it didn't already own
at $48.30/share cash (a deal that had valued MGM at $18B+). The
withdrawal removed the takeover-premium floor the stock had been trading
partly on. Same-day, BofA downgraded MGM to Neutral with a $40 price
target; this follows an earlier Morgan Stanley downgrade to Underweight
($33 target) citing a "reversion to the mean" in Las Vegas demand
post-Q1.

## Sentiment
Bearish/mixed. The proximate catalyst (bid withdrawal) is unambiguously
negative for near-term price support. Underlying analyst sentiment is
split: BofA's $40 target and Morgan Stanley's $33 target both now sit at
or near the post-drop price rather than well above it, and one
valuation-model source (GuruFocus GF Value) flags the stock as
~25.9% undervalued — but that's a single automated-model read, not
sell-side consensus, and should be weighted accordingly (lessons.md #5:
weigh a single data point below company-specific reported news). People
Inc. reportedly remains open to "strategic collaboration" short of a
buyout, which is a soft, undated signal, not a fresh catalyst.

## Risks
- The core event (bid withdrawal) is a removal of upside optionality,
  not new negative operating news — but it's still a real, confirmed,
  sizable repricing, not something to treat as automatically overdone.
- Two sell-side downgrades landed the same window citing genuine
  Las Vegas demand softness — a fundamentals concern independent of the
  M&A story.
- No dated near-term catalyst that would resolve either the demand
  question or reopen M&A speculation inside a 1-2 week window.
- Broader market was soft the same session (S&P -0.6%, Nasdaq -1.1%),
  so some portion of the move may be beta rather than purely
  MGM-specific — worth noting though the bid-withdrawal catalyst is
  clearly the dominant driver given the magnitude.
