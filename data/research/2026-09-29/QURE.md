## Catalyst
Real, dated, and decisively negative: uniQure released 48-month
(4-year) data from its Phase 1/2 AMT-130 Huntington's disease gene
therapy trial. The headline number — a 44% slowing of disease
progression on the cUHDRS composite measure — FAILED to reach
statistical significance. This is a significant reversal from the
company's own prior readout: one year ago, uniQure reported a 75%
slowing of disease progression at the 36-month mark from the same trial.
Compounding the disappointment, the FDA had already indicated that the
36-month data from 12 high-dose patients (not today's 4-year data) would
serve as the primary basis for the BLA submission under the accelerated
approval pathway — meaning today's weaker, non-significant result isn't
even the dataset the regulatory pathway depends on, leaving investors
uncertain what near-term regulatory value it adds.

## Sentiment
Sharply negative and immediate: shares fell as much as 62-64% in
premarket/intraday trading, among the largest single-day biotech declines
logged in this project. A securities-fraud investigation notice (Kehoe
Law Firm) was also filed same-day — standard plaintiff-firm activity
following a large stock drop, not independent evidence of wrongdoing, but
worth noting as a same-day development.

## Risks
This is a real, dated, negative catalyst on a long-only project — not
actionable regardless of magnitude or genuineness. The one relevant
forward consideration: yesterday's research note already flagged that
uniQure's cash position (~$810M, runway to 2030) meant the company wasn't
under near-term existential pressure — that fact is unchanged by today's
data, so this is a scientific/commercial setback, not (yet) a going-
concern event, though a ~60%+ market-cap loss in one day is a serious
blow to the BLA pathway's economics regardless.
