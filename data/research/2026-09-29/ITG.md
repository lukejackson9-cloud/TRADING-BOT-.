## Catalyst
ITG fell **-32.59%** from $10.48 (2026-09-25 close) to $7.065 (2026-09-28
close) — now down more than 50% from its $16 IPO price after less than 3
months of trading. **Important correction to the task's preliminary
framing**: this crash is NOT earnings-related. ITG's actual Q2 2026
results were reported 2026-08-12 (a month and a half before this move)
and were solid on their face — revenue $404.6M (+38% YoY), Adjusted
EBITDA $52.2M (+21% YoY, ahead of internal plan), NTM backlog $1.5B
(+21% YoY), FY26 guidance of $1.556B revenue / $202M EBITDA reaffirmed.
There is no new earnings print, guidance cut, or 8-K disclosing a
negative operational event dated to 2026-09-28 found in this search.

What the evidence actually points to: Seeking Alpha's same-day piece
("The September 28 Collapse Looks Like A Seller, Not A Broken Business")
attributes the drop to a **large shareholder exit** — i.e., forced or
opportunistic selling pressure, not new fundamental news. The timing
(~89 days after the July 1 IPO) is consistent with a standard ~90-day
lock-up expiration, the same mechanical event that hit SWMR today (see
SWMR.md) — plausible but NOT confirmed by any source found; no press
release or SEC filing documenting a lock-up expiration or secondary
offering dated 9/28 turned up in this search. Treating that explanation
as a reasonable inference, not a verified fact.

Real pre-existing fundamental concerns independent of today's move:
weak organic growth despite the 38% headline revenue growth (driven by
acquisitions), margin pressure, problematic cash conversion, and high
customer concentration (Comcast + Charter = 53% of Q2 revenue).

## Sentiment

## Risks
- **Unconfirmed catalyst**: the "shareholder exit" / lock-up explanation
  is the best-sourced read but not confirmed by a primary filing — a
  genuinely undocumented risk is a red flag on its own, not a case for
  buying a >50%-off-IPO stock.
- Extremely thin trading history (<3 months) — no way to judge what
  "normal" volatility or a real floor looks like for this name.
- Real customer concentration risk (53% from two customers) and weak
  organic growth/cash conversion, unresolved regardless of today's cause.
- A single bullish source (Seeking Alpha contributor) is exactly the
  weak-evidence pattern flagged in lessons.md #5 (a single analyst-style
  call is weaker evidence than company fundamentals) — here it's not
  even a rated sell-side analyst, just one commentator.
- Catching a falling knife on a structural/supply-driven crash (not a
  news-driven overreaction with a known resolution date) has no
  established edge in this project's evaluation work — same shape as the
  PSQL/de-SPAC "post-IPO pop-and-fade" pattern already PASSed repeatedly.
