## Catalyst
CLF (Cleveland-Cliffs, integrated steel producer) fell **-7.88%** from
$12.18 (2026-09-25 close) to $11.22 (2026-09-28 close) — WebSearch sources
put the same-day move at -8.6% to -9.4% intraday/afternoon prints,
consistent in direction but not exact magnitude with the price data given
(see lessons.md #2, noted not resolved). The driver is a real, dated,
negative company-specific event: CLF's wholly-owned Canadian subsidiary
**Stelco disclosed plans to indefinitely idle its cold-rolled and coated
steel operations at Hamilton Works** (Hamilton, Ontario), citing an
"unsustainable market" from U.S. tariff-driven trade disruption hitting
Canadian steel. Wind-down begins as early as October 9, with an estimated
350 steelworkers facing layoffs. Sources also note CLF's "two-sided"
tariff exposure — it benefits from Section 232 protection as a U.S.
producer but is hurt as a Canadian operator through Stelco — a
structural mismatch peers like Nucor and Steel Dynamics don't share, and
a heavier debt load amplifying the vulnerability. Next scheduled earnings
is 2026-10-19 (outside this project's ~2-week window).

## Sentiment
Bearish, and for a real fundamental reason, not sentiment/technical
noise. Nucor and Steel Dynamics also pulled back the same session but
by far less (~2%), meaning most of the move is CLF/Stelco-specific
operational bad news, not pure sector beta — a genuine negative catalyst.
One earlier-week note (GLJ Research raising PT to $17.48, Buy) is now
stale against this fresh negative print. Morgan Stanley/Wells Fargo hold
Hold ratings as of late September.

## Risks
- This is a **negative** catalyst — permanent-looking capacity idling and
  imminent layoffs, not a washed-out overreaction with an identifiable
  reason to expect a bounce.
- Long-only strategy has no way to act on a bearish thesis (can't short).
- Real balance-sheet risk (elevated debt) amplifying the tariff exposure,
  not a one-off headline.
- Next earnings (10/19) is outside the ~2-week trading window and could
  bring further guidance cuts tied to this same idling decision.
