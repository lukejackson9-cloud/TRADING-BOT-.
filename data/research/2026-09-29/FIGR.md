# FIGR — 2026-09-29

## Catalyst
Confirmed identity: FIGR = Figure Technology Solutions, Inc. (Reno, NV;
NASDAQ), a blockchain-based fintech lender (HELOCs, cash-out refi, DSCR
loans, crypto-backed loans, Figure Connect marketplace) — NOT the
private humanoid-robotics company "Figure AI." Confirmed distinct via
search (Figure Technology's own IPO/financials/analyst coverage all
reference the lending/blockchain-capital-markets business, no robotics
overlap). No single dated company-specific catalyst found for the
2026-09-28 -8.06% move ($31.90->$29.33, with intraday prints reported
as low as $29.77). Instead, sourcing points to a macro/sector move:
rising Treasury yields and shifting rate-cut expectations pressuring
rate-sensitive fintech/blockchain-lending names broadly, compounded by
a fresh Mizuho price-target cut and an earlier Weiss Ratings downgrade.
One source also mentions a "$1.6B lending milestone" announced around
the same window, which is framed as a positive that got swamped by the
broader volatility, not a negative driver. A short-seller report
(Morpheus Research, alleging FIGR doesn't actually originate loans
on-chain despite public claims) is real but dated to mid-April 2026 —
over 5 months stale, not a fresh 9/28 catalyst.

## Sentiment
Mixed-to-bearish on the day, but the broader analyst backdrop is
actually constructive: 7-analyst consensus is "Buy" with a $55.29
average 12-month target (well above current price), and BofA recently
upgraded FIGR to Neutral with target raised to $49. The 9/28 drop reads
as a rate-sensitivity/macro flinch on an otherwise well-regarded name,
not a repudiation of the business.

## Risks
- No company-specific dated catalyst identified for this exact move —
  per lessons.md #3, a move driven by macro/rate conditions rather than
  a company-specific event is weaker grounds for a directional trade
  either way.
- The stale April short-seller allegation (loan origination claims) is
  a real overhang but is not fresh news and shouldn't be treated as
  today's catalyst (lessons.md #6 — don't recycle an old narrative as if
  new).
- Blockchain-lending business model still carries real regulatory/
  disclosure scrutiny risk (the Morpheus allegation specifically).
- Rate-sensitive name — further hawkish rate repricing could keep
  pressuring it independent of company fundamentals.
