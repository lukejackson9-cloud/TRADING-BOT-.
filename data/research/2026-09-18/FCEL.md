## Catalyst
On 2026-09-16 the US House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417-3,
requiring large (100MW+) data centers to bear their own power-generation
and grid-infrastructure costs rather than socializing them onto
residential/commercial ratepayers. Markets read this as bullish for
behind-the-meter power suppliers, and FCEL (+14.26%, close $17.71),
Plug Power (+7%) and Bloom Energy (+3%) all moved together on 09-17 —
this is a real, dated, legislative catalyst, but it lifted the whole
fuel-cell/behind-the-meter group at once, not FCEL specifically.
Company-specific context on top: Citigroup initiated coverage 09-17
with a Neutral rating and $19 PT (roughly in-line, not bullish); broader
analyst consensus is Moderate Buy, mean PT $22.67 (~28% upside).

## Sentiment
Mixed-to-cautiously-bullish. Broad Moderate Buy consensus and a sector
tailwind, but the one same-day analyst action (Citi) was Neutral, not a
buy call, and GuruFocus flags the stock as overvalued on its GF Value
model after the rally.

## Risks
- Sector-wide move (see lessons.md #3) — not an independent FCEL signal;
  correlate with any OKLO/PLUG/BE ideas reaching council the same day.
- Active securities class action covering purchasers 2026-06-24 to
  2026-09-01, alleging inadequate disclosure of manufacturing shortfalls,
  production costs, weak output, delayed deliveries, and potential
  charges under the Fit Energy agreement — a real, unresolved legal
  overhang, not rumor.
- Documented company-specific history of earnings misses and dilution
  (see 09-03 PASS entry) — this legislative catalyst doesn't resolve
  those underlying issues.
- Legislative catalyst is a House vote only; still needs the Senate and
  President to become law — real but early-stage, not an enacted policy
  change yet.
