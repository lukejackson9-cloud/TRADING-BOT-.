# OXM — 2026-09-07

## Catalyst
Real, dated, and well-documented — but bearish, not bullish. Oxford
Industries (parent of Tommy Bahama, Lilly Pulitzer, Johnny Was) reported
Q2 FY2026 results on 2026-09-03. Headline numbers were mixed: net sales of
$394.4M (down 2.2% YoY, near the high end of guidance) and adjusted EPS of
$1.34 (up from $1.26 a year ago, boosted by a one-time $42.0M tariff
refund worth $2.07/share of the $3.25 GAAP EPS). The real driver of the
selloff was a full-year guidance cut: full-year adjusted EPS guidance was
lowered to $1.60-$2.00 (from $2.11 prior year and well below the ~$2.61
consensus), with Q3 guidance implying an adjusted loss per share of
$1.20-$1.40. Lilly Pulitzer sales fell 5.6% and Johnny Was fell 8.8%,
with management citing "more significant than anticipated" assortment and
marketing problems at Lilly Pulitzer plus broader consumer macro pressure.
Tommy Bahama, the largest brand, was roughly flat (+0.8%).

## Sentiment
Bearish. This is a clean earnings-miss-and-guide-down story: the company
itself cut forward guidance well below consensus and flagged unresolved,
worse-than-expected problems at a specific brand. The tariff-refund boost
to GAAP EPS makes the headline beat somewhat misleading — the adjusted,
recurring picture is weaker.

## Risks
- This is a falling-knife pattern immediately post-guidance-cut; catching
  a "bounce" here has no confirmed catalyst yet (no analyst
  capitulation-then-upgrade cycle found in search results).
- Consumer discretionary/apparel exposure — broader macro consumer
  pressure was explicitly cited as a headwind by management, not just a
  company-specific issue.
- Lilly Pulitzer's problems were described as "more significant than
  anticipated" — i.e., already surprised management once this cycle,
  raising the odds of another disappointment before it stabilizes.
- Next quarter's guidance already implies a loss (Q3 adjusted loss per
  share of $1.20-$1.40) — the near-term setup is for continued weak
  prints, not a quick reversal.
